RookStudio is fastchess-gui grown into a program for your own games as well as engine tournaments: databases,
notation written in place, an opening tree and repertoires, Lichess, and an assistant that comments games on your
machine. This first preview is private.

## Views

- Up to three views of your own, switched at the top of the rail (Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+3): each with its pages, name, icon
  and colour, and its Board, for study or for the engines' analysis. The first start offers *Tests*, *Study* and
  *Everything*, and copies a fastchess-gui profile found on the machine if you wish; fastchess-gui stays as it is.
- The Home page makes cards of the view's pages: the game to resume, what to start, the recent games, your
  databases, Lichess and what runs.

## Databases

- Databases of millions of games, imported in the background from PGN, `.zst`, `.gz` or `.zip` files (11.7 million
  games in about 20 seconds on 8 cores), a tournament beside them losing no time. A damaged game is kept up to the move
  that cannot be read, and the file is shown at its line.
- The game list sorts and filters at once; the search panel finds games by their headers, a position, a material
  balance or a pattern of men, and keeps a search by its name for any database.
- The tournaments of the profile and the PGN file on the board are read as databases, without a copy.

## The study Board

- The notation written in place: comments before and after a move, symbols typed with `!` and `?`, a variation made by
  playing another move, moved up or deleted, each change undone with Ctrl+Z. A move can be typed by its letters.
- Arrows and coloured squares with the right button, in 2D and in 3D; the headers unfolded in place (Alt+H); Ctrl+S
  saves a game where it belongs, a menu offering a database or a file for the others; Ctrl+Tab the recent games;
  Ctrl+← and Ctrl+→ the previous and the next game of the list.
- Five tabs: Notation, Analysis, Tree, References (the strongest games of the position) and Questions.

## Openings

- The Tree tab: the moves of the position in your databases, your Polyglot books and Lichess's explorer, with their
  scores, average Elo, performance and last year played; the novelty of the game's line, confirmed by a position
  search.
- Repertoires: a star in the Tree tab marks a move; the Openings page measures a repertoire's coverage on your
  databases and lists its gaps, the most often met first.

## Lichess

- An account linked without a password, in the browser, and revoked when it is unlinked; a player's games imported
  and synced into a database.
- Lichess's cloud evaluation and its 7-piece tables in the Analysis tab, each asked for at its first use, their answers
  kept in the profile.
- The official broadcasts followed live in a mosaic, each game analysed by your engine.

## The assistant

- Questions about the position or the game, answered from what your engine, your databases and the endgame tables
  establish; a comment on a move or on each key moment, marked as the assistant's; the review of a game in words: its
  key moments, its story and what to work on.
- Its model (RookStudio commentary v11, about 2.7 GB, downloaded once) runs on the machine, on the GPU through Vulkan
  or on the processor; only its download goes through the Internet. It writes in English or in French.

## Engines

- Popular engines: ten of the strongest, downloaded from their authors' GitHub releases in the build this processor
  runs, checked and added in a click, their new versions offered once a day.
- The engine tournaments of fastchess-gui 0.6.8, unchanged, except that the engines' logos in Live and on the big
  screen are now optional, off by default (Settings > Appearance > Logos).

## Languages

- The interface in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian and Simplified Chinese,
  and the user guide too, with its new chapters on the study side, opened by F1 at the section of what is under the
  mouse.
