RookStudio's second preview brings ChessBase databases and CTG books, games to download from their sources, the
training of a repertoire, theory studies, several engines side by side, more of Lichess, and an assistant that tells
the story of a game in its own words. It is still private.

## Databases and sources

- ChessBase databases copied into a RookStudio database: CBH and 2CBH, and their archives (`.cbv` and `.2cbv`, `.cbz`
  and `.2cbz` with their password). Dropped on the Databases page or chosen with *Import a file…*, a database first
  shows what comes in and what stays out (training questions, pictures, sounds and videos, links, texts), then is copied
  in the background, what stayed out counted at the end. Copied again, only its new games come in; its files are only
  read.
- *From a source…* on the Databases page: TWIC's weekly issues, offered every Monday once one is added, with a report of
  their tournaments and players; a month of Lichess's games, filtered while it downloads (both players at 2400 or more,
  no bullet, to begin with); a month of Lichess's broadcasts; a chess.com player's games, followed if you wish; Lumbra's
  Gigabase, read from its `.7z` as it is. The sources you follow sit under the list of databases, and their news on the
  Home page.
- PGN files in `.7z` archives imported like the others.
- FIDE cards for the players of a database (title, federation, ratings), asked of Lichess only when you open one.

## Openings

- CTG books in the Books page and as a source of the Tree tab, each move with its mark, its colour and its games; *For
  engines* makes a Polyglot copy of one.
- A repertoire trained with spaced repetition (FSRS): the positions due today, then new ones, those most met in your
  databases first. Each answer is judged by itself, right or wrong and the time it took, with no button to press.
- Theory studies: annotated PGN files added to the theory, read where they are and read again when they change, their
  notes shown under the moves in the Tree tab.
- In the Tree tab, each move of Lichess's players month by month, and the games the explorer names, opened on the board.

## Analysis and the assistant

- Up to four engines side by side in the Analysis tab, each with its colour, its arrow and its card, one total of
  threads shared between them.
- Lichess's evaluations kept offline: 418 million positions, about 6 GB on the disk from a monthly file of 22.5 GB read
  as it downloads, give an evaluation at once wherever Lichess has one, in the Analysis tab, the review and the Tree
  tab, without anything sent over the network.
- The review of a game is the same each time: each position is searched for a number of nodes, from a fresh start, by
  four engine processes side by side by default (fewer beside a tournament), so that a long game is reviewed in a few
  minutes and two reviews of it give the same verdicts, key moments and comments. Where the move played was the only
  good one, the key moment says so with the next best move beside it.
- The story of a game written by the assistant's model, in English or in French, the templates' version a click away.
  Each sentence, the model's or the templates', is checked against the board it speaks of: a sentence of the model
  refused is written again, one of the templates left out.
- On a move that already has a comment (yours, Lichess's analysis, an annotator's), the assistant's comes beside it,
  never in its place.

## Lichess

- Your ratings, figures and activity on the Home page, one card per time control.
- A Lichess link pasted with Ctrl+V on the Board or the Databases page brings its game, study, broadcast, tournament,
  player or bookmarks.
- Fair play wherever you play: the engines, the tree, the tables and the assistant stay locked on the position of a game
  you are playing on Lichess, correspondence included. A finished game comes in with Lichess's analysis, its review
  offered at once.
- Chosen players' games followed live in the mosaic; broadcasts with their standings, whole tournaments followed, and a
  search.
- Preparing a game against an opponent: their games, their openings, and your games against them.
- Your Lichess studies, read, imported into a database or followed, and a game sent into one.
- *lichess.org…* under the board: open the position there, share the game, have a position played out, bookmark it.

## Engines

- An engine tournament shown live on a Lichess broadcast of your own.
- Opening suites for engine matches made from Lichess's named openings or from the positions its players analysed.

## Languages

- What is new comes in the eight languages of the interface and of the guide.
