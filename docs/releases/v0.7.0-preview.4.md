RookStudio's fourth preview is about what you make of your games: the maintenance of your databases, searches by
pattern, manoeuvre and pawn structure, a player's dossier, the opening report and tournaments' crosstables, then pages,
diagrams, GIFs and PDFs to share them. On the Board, every move of a game explained, a guided visit of its key moments
and the exact verdict of the tablebases. On Lichess, arenas and Swiss tournaments, followed and played, and one of your
engines offered to lichess.org's analysis board. The repository is public from this preview on.

## Databases

- Duplicate games found in a database: the same players, year and moves, or one the start of the other (a broadcast
  cut short), even from two sources; each pair shown side by side with what differs, merged into the fullest of the
  two, or both kept. A source's games imported again are recognised as such.
- Players' names brought together: the spellings of one player (initials, accents, transliterations) gathered by the
  FIDE ids their games give and by FIDE's rating list, downloaded on request; each choice explained, undone if you
  prefer, written into the games only when you ask.
- Check and compact: every segment of a database read again, every game replayed and compared, in the background; the
  room of deleted games given back, the old files kept until RookStudio closes so that a compaction can be undone.
- A database whose segment no longer reads (a file cut short, missing or changed while RookStudio was closed) opens with
  its other segments; *Keep what can be read* sets the damaged games aside, and *Import their file again* brings them
  back from their file.

## Search

- Patterns in a small language with the words of CQL (men and squares, attacks, defends, pins, en prise, passed pawns,
  checks and mates, lines of moves), written by hand or built with menus; a mistake is shown with its line and the word
  it expected.
- Manoeuvres: the route of a piece, a knight from c3 to d5 through e2 and f4, in so many moves at most, from the start
  or from the Board's position.
- Similar pawn structures: the games whose pawns stood like those of the Board's position, by name (Carlsbad,
  hedgehog, isolated queen's pawn, Maróczy…) or by likeness.

## Reports

- A player's dossier from your databases, opened from their name: their openings with White and Black and the gaps
  your repertoires leave against them, their rating over the years and their results against stronger and weaker
  players, their pawn structures, their last games and yours against them; matched with FIDE's rating list.
- The opening report of a position: its moves and main lines with their figures and an evaluation, the pawn
  structures and plans played from it (*The minority attack: b4 then b5*), its key games and its novelties, from your
  databases and, with a linked account, Lichess's explorer.
- A tournament's crosstable and standings from a database's games: round robin, Swiss or matches, guessed and
  changeable; the tie-breaks of FIDE's rules, each player's performance and rating change; a Swiss kept from Lichess
  with its pairing file.

## Publishing

- A game as a replayable HTML page, a diagram as PNG or SVG, a passage as an animated GIF, a game, a dossier, a report
  or a crosstable printed to PDF: all made by RookStudio on your machine, in your board's colours, with its arrows and
  coloured squares.

## Analysis

- Every move of a game explained: the theory grouped by opening name, the simplest moves in a word (*Forced
  recapture.*), the others in a sentence the local assistant writes from the facts of the position, which a checker
  reads before it is shown (templates when the model is not there), in English or French.
- A guided visit of a game's key moments, each a question (find the move), and "Why not?" for any move you try instead.
- Practical moves: among the moves the engine finds nearly as good as its best, the one that scores best against a
  human of the level you choose (in Lichess's games or yours), or that leaves the fewest good replies; never a losing
  move.
- The Endgames tab: the tablebases' exact verdict of the position and of each move, from your Syzygy tables or
  Lichess's 7-piece tables online; a tournament's endings checked against them.
- Moves typed in the piece letters of your language (C, D, T, F in French, S, D, T, L in German…), the English ones
  always read too.

## Lichess

- Arenas and Swiss tournaments: the list of those in progress and to come; one followed in the mosaic with its standing
  and the games of its first eight; joined and played on the Board, Berserk included, every aid locked between two games
  until you pause; a Swiss's round announced, a finished Swiss kept with its crosstable.
- One of your engines in lichess.org's analysis board: it computes on your machine (or one of your remote machines) and
  its lines show there, for your account only. A tournament comes first, the service pauses during your live game, and
  the positions of your correspondence games are never analysed.

## Engines

- An engine taken from *Popular engines…* keeps each version in a folder of its own; once an update replaced one, it is
  deleted when no tournament that is not over plays it, and its card lists the versions on the disk, with *Keep … too*.
- From fastchess-gui 0.6.9 and 0.6.10: Live's engine cards keep their height through a game, the overview's Elo chart
  comes right under the standings whatever the number of engines, the mosaic keeps its boards as a tournament's last
  games end, and a stopped tournament opened from the list resumes whatever the size of its journal.

## Languages

- What is new comes in the eight languages of the interface and of the guide.
