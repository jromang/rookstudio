RookStudio's third preview is about training and playing: a Training page with Lichess's puzzles, your own mistakes,
endgames against the tablebases, mates and board vision; a Play page for games against an engine at the strength you
choose and for your games on Lichess; one of your engines as a Lichess bot; Lichess TV in the mosaic. It is still
private.

## Training

- A Training page of its own, and one review a day: *Review all* goes through what is due today, one session after the
  other on the Board: the puzzles you missed, your mistakes, the endgames you missed and the positions of your
  repertoires. The Home page counts them.
- Lichess's puzzles offline: its 6.2 million puzzles downloaded in one click (295 MB) and kept in about 300 MB, by
  theme, opening and rating, ready while they download. A series of 20 at your level, of a theme or an opening, never a
  puzzle already seen; a hint, the solution move by move, the original game on lichess.org. A puzzle missed comes back
  the next day. Your rating (Glicko-2) starts from your Lichess puzzle rating when an account is linked.
- Lichess's puzzles online, with your account: a batch of 50 of the theme and difficulty chosen, played offline too,
  your answers sent to Lichess at its end, rated or not.
- Your mistakes as exercises: the positions where you went wrong in your own games (found by your names, set in
  Settings, and your linked Lichess account), taken from their review when the better move is clear; any answer is
  judged by the review's engine, as the review weighs a move. Your last games not yet reviewed are reviewed in the
  background on request.
- Guessing the moves of a game: a game drawn from a database (a minimum rating, the list's quick filter) or the one on
  the Board, played from one side; the theory moves of your databases go by themselves; 3 points for the game's move,
  2 or 1 for a move nearly as good, the moves that cost the most at the end.
- Endgames against the tablebases: 18 of them, from the basic mates to Lucena, Philidor and Vancura, the opposition and
  the square of the pawn, each start checked against the tables. The defender resists as long as it can; a mistake
  shows the moves that kept the result and the idea of the endgame. The tables come from your disk, from the 3 to 5
  piece Syzygy tables downloaded on request (938 MB), or from Lichess online up to 7 pieces. Your own games' endgames
  too.
- Mates: 20 of Lichess's mate puzzles, in 1 to 5 moves or by pattern (back rank, smothered, Anastasia, Arabian, Boden,
  double bishop, hook).
- Board vision: find or name a square in 30 seconds, from White's side, Black's or both; a blindfold game against an
  engine at 1000, 1500 or 2000; a line read in your head, then a question on a square it changed.

## Play

- A Play page, and a game against an engine of your library: at a rating it allows, at a number of nodes per move or at
  full strength; its openings from its own book, your databases, your repertoire (it plays what you prepared against),
  a player's games or a Polyglot or CTG book; a handicap (the f-pawn, the b-knight, the a-rook or the queen taken from
  the engine); takebacks and three hints, counted. The analysis, the tree and the assistant stay hidden until the end
  (*Show them* marks the game). A game left when RookStudio closes waits for you at the next launch. At the end, its
  review, a rematch with the colours reversed, and your results against that engine at that level; the games kept in a
  database of their own.
- Your games on Lichess, from the same page: seek a rapid, classical or correspondence game, challenge a player (blitz
  too), answer a challenge received wherever you are in RookStudio. The game is played on the Board with Lichess's
  clocks and chat; live, a move made during the opponent's turn waits for yours; by correspondence, a move goes on
  Enter. Fair play holds: every aid locked during a live game; by correspondence the engine, the cloud evaluation, the
  tables and the assistant locked, your databases and books open. A network cut, or RookStudio closed during a game:
  the game comes back by itself.

## Lichess

- Lichess TV in the mosaic, without an account: the Lichess page shows each channel's featured game (Top rated, Bullet,
  Blitz, Rapid, Classical, UltraBullet, Bots, Computer); a channel's games in progress fill the mosaic, each analysed by
  an engine of yours, a finished game giving its place to another, the moves three moves late as Lichess shows them to
  anyone. A game opened on the Board is followed, and when it ends the Board goes on to the channel's next featured game
  unless you stay on it; a game can be kept in a database at its end. Your own games never show, and the mosaic's
  engines rest during your live game.
- A followed player's game is now followed to its end on lichess.org: its stream sends the game again from its start
  when it opens, which was taken for a break, and the game was given up.

## Engines

- One of your engines as a Lichess bot, from its card on the Engines page: a new Lichess account of its own (RookStudio
  creates none), linked with the right to play as a bot only and checked first (never your own account, never one that
  has played), then upgraded once you type its name, as Lichess asks. It accepts the challenges you choose (unrated at
  first, blitz and rapid, one game at a time, widened on its card) and declines the others with Lichess's reason. It
  plays while RookStudio is open, a tournament first: when the tournament needs the cores, it declines. Its game in
  progress is followed on the Board with its engine's search, its finished games kept in a database named after it.
- An engine's card opens Lichess TV's Bots and Computer channels in the mosaic: Lichess's best bots, or players against
  its AI, that engine analysing each game.
- A tournament game opened on the Board shows each engine's evaluation and depth beside its move, the rest on hover
  (time, nodes, speed, tablebase hits, win, draw and loss chances, the line it expected); the Summary draws the engines'
  curve from the start, without an analysis. The engines' comments are kept as they were read and written back
  unchanged, in fastchess's style and in the other common style.
